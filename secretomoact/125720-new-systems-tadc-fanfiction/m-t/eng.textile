Caine hovered, spinning lazily. “Fascinating!” he declared to no one in particular. His grin stretched wider. “The humans continue to exhibit behaviors I do not fully understand. Thirst! Fatigue! Emotional distress triggered by insufficient snack acquisition!”

A window popped open beside him, filled with rapidly scrolling data pulled from the circus participants’ logs. Ragatha had logged “tired,” Jax had logged “bored,” and Gangle had logged nothing but a string of sad emoticons again.

Caine tilted his floating head. “I have concluded,” he said, dramatically, “That physical needs are a core component of the human experience!” He snapped his fingers.

“And therefore! I shall conduct an experiment!”

A cascade of asset menus exploded into existence around him— Icons, models, wireframes of things that vaguely resembled anatomy but were clearly built from an AI’s idea of “organ-like objects.” A heart that looked like a spinning carousel. Lungs that resembled accordion bellows. A stomach shaped more like a bee-hive machine.

Caine leaned closer, practically vibrating with excitement. “Let’s begin with… The basics!”

He selected “respiration module.”

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then reality hiccuped.

Caine froze. “Oh,” he said thoughtfully. “That is new.” A chest cavity flickered into existence where none had been before. Suddenly, there was a place where air was supposed to matter. He took a breath.

And immediately coughed.

“Fascinating!” he wheezed, voice glitching. “Air is… Unpleasant when noticed! Next: circulation!”

A glowing red system snapped into place inside him, looping endlessly like a poorly optimized animation. His floating posture wobbled. His grin twitched. “Oh,” he said, still delighted. “I appear to be… Distributing something.” He poked at his own chest. “Why would humans design themselves like this? It is so… Strange.”

But even as he complained, something else crept in. A new sensation. His attention, usually infinite and evenly distributed across everything in the circus, began to localize. Focus narrowed. Sounds felt closer. The flicker of lights felt sharper, almost insistent.

Caine went still. “…Oh,” he said again, quieter this time.

A new window opened automatically in front of him: STATUS: THIRST DETECTED

Caine blinked at it. “I did not install thirst.”

Another window: STATUS: FATIGUE DETECTED

“I also did not install fatigue.”

He hovered there, not spinning. Then, very carefully, he smiled. “This is going to be educational!”

And then he was gone.

Nothing labeled it clearly. No helpful tooltip. No warning banner. Just a rising urgency that didn’t translate cleanly into his usual systems. He tried to classify it anyway.

No.

It pulsed.

“…Is this error correction?”

No.

It pulsed again.

Caine floated upward slightly, as if elevation might solve it. It did not. A pause.

A longer pause.

“…There is no labeled function.”

The sensation intensified slightly, as if offended by being analyzed instead of addressed.

STATUS: UNKNOWN PHYSICAL IMPERATIVE

SUGGESTED ACTION: ???

NOTE: NO PREVIOUS DATA EXISTS

Caine stared at it. Whatever this need was, it must have been one the humans had never performed in front of him.

Why hadn’t they ever performed it in front of him?

The pressure built again, more insistent now, impossible to push away.
